# FAVEL Dataset (Favel-DS)

The **FAVEL Dataset** is designed for the evaluation of automated fact-validation and fact-checking algorithms over Knowledge Graphs. All facts are based on the DBpedia snapshot (`dbpedia-snapshot-2022-03-release`) and are entirely in English.

The dataset is a collection of [RDF](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-primer/) models, where each model contains a singular fact and its corresponding truth value. 

## 📥 Downloads and Links
* **GitHub Repository (Train/Test splits & auxiliary files):** [dice-group/favel](https://github.com/dice-group/favel/tree/main/Datasets/Favel_Dataset)
* **Combined Dataset (.ttl format):** [Zenodo Record](https://zenodo.org/records/15390036)
* **Student Evaluation Set:** A specialized test file *without* ground truth labels is provided specifically for educational purposes and student evaluation.

## 📊 Dataset Structure & Usage

The dataset is structured into a **train set** and a **test set** across 11 relations. The training and test sets are divided randomly in a ratio of 70:30, respectively. 

Typically, the train set should be used to fit your fact-checking algorithm, and the test set to evaluate its performance.

| # | Property | Description |
|--|--|--|
|1| Movie - Director | Person who is the director of the movie |
|2| Movie - Producer | Person who is the producer of the movie|
|3| Movie - Production Company | Company that is producing the movie |
|4| Movie - Starring | Person who is starring in the movie |
|5| Scientist - Academic Discipline | Academic discipline/specialization to which a scientist belongs |
|6| Scientist - Award | The awards received by the scientist|
|7| Scientist - Deathplace | The scientist's place of death |
|8| Scientist - Birthplace | The scientist's place of birth |
|9| University - Affiliation | The organization with which the university is affiliated |
|10| University - Chancellor | The chancellor of the university |
|11| University - City | The city in which the university is located |

## 🏗️ How the Dataset was Created

### Positive Data
The positive data was collected directly from DBpedia. For each relation, we issued a SPARQL query and extracted the top 50 results. We collected a total of 550 correct facts (385 in the train set and 165 in the test set). 

### Negative Data
The negative data was generated by corrupting the previously extracted positive data. 

Assume a triple `(s, p, o)` in a knowledge base `K`. We extracted triples `(s, p', o')` such that `p` and `p'` belong to the same domain and have a close range. 

**Example:** 
For the relation `movie-director`, `p` is `dbo:director`, which has the domain `dbo:Film` and range `dbo:Person`. To create negative examples, we extracted results with `p'` being `dbo:Person`, where `(s, p, o)` is not the same as `(s, p', o')`. 
* **Correct:** `<http://dbpedia.org/resource/Dead_Snow> dbo:director <http://dbpedia.org/resource/Tommy_Wirkola>.` → `(s, p, o)`
* **Wrong Entity:** `<http://dbpedia.org/resource/Dead_Snow> dbo:Person <http://dbpedia.org/resource/Ane_Dahl_Torp>.` → `(s, p', o')`

The negative examples are then stored as `(s, p, o')`, such that `(s, p, o')` does not actually exist in the knowledge graph.
* **Generated Negative Fact:** `<http://dbpedia.org/resource/Dead_Snow> dbo:director <http://dbpedia.org/resource/Ane_Dahl_Torp>.` → `(s, p, o')`

We generated a total of 550 incorrect facts (385 in the train set and 165 in the test set).
